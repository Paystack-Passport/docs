Changes that affect how you integrate: new endpoints and fields, behavior changes, and new error
or failure codes. Breaking changes are called out explicitly.

<Update label="2026-10-08" tags={["New feature"]}>
  ## Pay businesses over PayShap by ShapName

  A PayShap beneficiary can now be addressed by a **ShapName** as well as a mobile number. A
  ShapName is the `name@bank` identifier that businesses usually register for PayShap, for example
  `acme@nedbank`. Put it in `account_number`, exactly as the recipient gives it, including the part
  after `@`:

  ```json
  {
    "type": "fiat",
    "name": "Acme Trading",
    "label": "Acme supplier",
    "currency": "ZAR",
    "country_code": "ZA",
    "network": "bank",
    "rail": "payshap",
    "account_number": "acme@nedbank"
  }
  ```

  Everything else works as it does for a mobile number:

  - We resolve the ShapName before we create the beneficiary. For a business, `account_name` is
    the registered business name.
  - An unregistered ShapName returns [`400 invalid_input`](/errors#invalid_input), and no
    beneficiary is created.
  - A value that cannot be a ShapName also returns `400 invalid_input`: nothing before or after
    the `@`, more than one `@`, or a space inside it.

  Mobile-number proxies are unchanged.
</Update>

<Update label="2026-10-07" tags={["Behavior change"]}>
  ## Failed transactions now tell you why: `quote_expired`, `refund_failed`, `returned`

  A conversion, onramp, or offramp that ended badly used to report one thing no matter what
  happened: `failure_reason: "failed"`, *"This transaction could not be completed."* A quote nobody
  funded, which simply expired, was indistinguishable from something breaking mid-processing — which
  inflated apparent failure rates and misrouted alerting. Three new codes separate them.

  | `failure_reason` | When |
  | --- | --- |
  | `quote_expired` | The payment never arrived before the quote expired. No funds moved. |
  | `refund_failed` | The transaction failed, and the refund attempt did not complete. |
  | `returned` | The transaction was returned and any funds debited for it have been released. |

  **`quote_expired` is the common one** — by a wide margin. It means nobody paid in time, not that
  anything went wrong on our side. No funds moved, so it is always safe to create a fresh quote and
  try again. If you alert on failures, this is the code to exclude.

  **This can change what you already receive.** Cases that previously arrived as `failed` now arrive
  as `quote_expired`, `refund_failed`, or `returned`. Nothing was removed and no field changed shape
  — but if you branch on `failure_reason === "failed"`, that branch will stop catching them. `failed`
  remains the generic catch-all for anything we do not surface specifically.

  **`returned` is the one that is not a `failed` status.** The other codes accompany
  `status: "failed"`; `returned` accompanies `status: "returned"`, which is its own terminal state.
  If you only read `failure_reason` when `status` is `"failed"`, you will not see it — gate on the
  transaction not having succeeded instead.

  Note that `returned` deliberately does not promise a credit. Whether funds come back depends on how
  far the transaction got: an offramp whose payout had already settled is credited back in full,
  while one that never debited you has nothing to return. Check your balance or transactions list
  rather than inferring a credit from the code.

  Statuses themselves are unchanged — an expired conversion still reports `status: "failed"`, so the
  six status values stay exactly as documented. The distinction travels on `failure_reason` alone,
  which is why this needed no new field.

  The full list, with the exact `failure_reason_message` for each, is in
  [Failure reasons](/api-reference/failure-reasons).
</Update>

<Update label="2026-10-06" tags={["Behavior change"]}>
  ## South African account numbers are now validated before payout

  A ZAR bank `account_number` that cannot structurally belong to its bank is now rejected with
  `400 invalid_input`, instead of being accepted and failing at the bank days later. The check is
  local to us — no extra latency, no new field, no schema change.

  **This can reject requests that previously succeeded.** Two places:

  - `POST /v1/beneficiaries` and `PATCH /v1/beneficiaries/{id}` — at the point the details are
    captured, so the person entering them can still fix it.
  - `POST /v1/quotes` for an offramp — which means **a beneficiary saved before today can fail
    here**, even though it saved cleanly at the time. That is deliberate: the quote is the last
    moment we hold the number before it goes to the payout network. Update the beneficiary's
    `account_number` and quote again.

  Two rules, and neither is a reformatting:

  - **Digits only.** Separators are rejected, not stripped — `6200-4626-662` is invalid even
    though removing the dashes would leave a valid number. We never alter what you send, so a
    number we accept is exactly the number we pay. Trim your inputs before sending them.
  - **Length must match the bank** on the beneficiary's `account_provider` — FNB 11, Capitec 10,
    Nedbank 10, Standard Bank 9 or 11, Absa 8–11, and so on; any other South African bank is
    checked against 8–11 digits. The full table is in
    [Errors → South African account numbers](/errors#south-african-account-numbers).

  The `message` names the bank and both digit counts — `FNB accounts are 11 digits; this account
  number has 10.` — and is written to be shown to whoever entered the value. `"Invalid account"`
  becomes a support ticket; the specific string gets fixed.

  **PayShap is unaffected.** On the `payshap` rail `account_number` is an E.164 proxy, not a bank
  account number, so none of these rules apply to it.

  **What this does not do:** it cannot tell you whether an account exists, is open, or accepts
  credits. A structurally perfect number can still be returned by the bank as unrecognised or
  closed. This only removes the failures that were impossible from the start.
</Update>

<Update label="2026-09-30" tags={["New feature"]}>
  ## Pay South African beneficiaries over PayShap

  Offramp beneficiaries in `ZAR` can now be paid over **PayShap**, South Africa's instant-payment
  scheme. A beneficiary has a new optional field, `rail`, which says **how** money reaches the
  account. `network` still says **what** the account is. A PayShap beneficiary is a bank account
  (`network: "bank"`) that you pay with `rail: "payshap"`.

  You address a PayShap beneficiary by its **proxy**, the recipient's mobile number in E.164 form.
  Put the proxy in `account_number`:

  ```json
  {
    "type": "fiat",
    "name": "J Doe",
    "currency": "ZAR",
    "country_code": "ZA",
    "network": "bank",
    "rail": "payshap",
    "account_number": "+27821234567"
  }
  ```

  **Do not send `provider_id`.** A proxy resolves to the bank that holds it, so you do not choose a
  provider. If you send one, we ignore it. PayShap beneficiaries return `provider: null`.

  **Do not send `account_name`.** We resolve the proxy to its registered holder before we create
  the beneficiary, and we store that name. Any name you send is replaced. This is the reason to
  prefer a proxy to a raw account number: we can verify who holds a proxy before you pay it.

  If we cannot verify the proxy, we reject the request with
  [`400 invalid_input`](/errors#invalid_input) and create no beneficiary. This happens when the
  number is not registered for PayShap, and also when the recipient's bank does not answer in time.
  You cannot tell the two cases apart, so retry once before you treat a number as invalid. If
  PayShap itself is unavailable, you get
  [`500 external_service_unavailable`](/errors#external_service_unavailable); retry later.

  `rail: "payshap"` is valid only with `network: "bank"`, currency `ZAR` and country `ZA`. Any
  other combination returns [`400 invalid_input`](/errors#invalid_input). If PayShap is switched
  off, creating a PayShap beneficiary returns [`403 forbidden`](/errors#forbidden).

  ### What changes in responses

  - **Beneficiaries** echo `details.rail` when it is set. Beneficiaries without a rail do not get
    the field.
  - **Offramps** paid over PayShap report `payment_instrument.type` as `"bank_account"`, the same
    value as any bank payout, with `payment_instrument.rail: "payshap"` beside it. If you branch
    on `type`, you do not need to change anything.
  - **`provider` can be `null`** on a fiat beneficiary's `details` and on `payment_instrument`,
    but only when `rail` is `"payshap"`. If you read `provider.id` or `provider.name`, handle
    `null` before you create PayShap beneficiaries.
</Update>

<Update label="2026-09-30" tags={["Documentation", "Behavior change"]}>
  ## Every webhook event is now documented, with payloads

  [Webhook events](/webhooks/events) is a new reference covering all 27 events we emit: which
  category each belongs to, exactly when it fires, and a complete example payload per family —
  onramp, offramp, conversion, transfer, deposit and verification.

  Nothing about the events themselves changed. If you have been reverse-engineering payloads from
  deliveries, the page confirms the shape and the guarantees: `event_object` mirrors the matching
  REST resource, plus event-only fields (`actual_source_amount`, `actual_destination_amount`,
  `actual_rate`, `terminal_at`, `deposit_timestamp`) that the REST body does not carry. Reconcile
  money against the `actual_*` values, not the quoted ones.

  A few behaviours the page pins down that were easy to get wrong:

  - A **held deposit sends two events** — `deposit.held`, then `deposit.confirmed` on release.
  - A **cross-currency deposit never reaches a terminal `deposit.*` event**. It stays at
    `received`; the `conversion.*` events carry the outcome.
  - `funds_received` and `processing` are **separate** events on the FX families, and a resource
    emits any given event **at most once** — so a cancelled-then-reversed transfer reaches you as a
    single `transfer.failed`.

  ### Removed: `kyc.expired`

  The `kyc` category previously registered a `kyc.expired` topic that nothing ever emitted — no
  endpoint has ever received one. It has been removed, so the subscribable list now matches what we
  actually send. Verification events are `kyc.approved` and `kyc.rejected`; an expired verification
  link is visible on `GET /v1/customers/{customerId}/verifications`, and the customer can request a
  new one. No action needed unless you wrote a handler branch for an event you never received.
</Update>

<Update label="2026-09-24" tags={["New feature", "Fix"]}>
  ## Cross-currency deposits now tell you when the money arrives

  Deposits on a [cross-currency credential](/api-reference/auto-conversion) used to be silent
  until the conversion started — your first webhook was `conversion.created`, and if the
  conversion never started, you heard nothing at all.

  They now emit `deposit.received` on arrival. The event waits for the FX quote, so it carries
  what the deposit is worth in your wallet's currency rather than just the amount the payer sent:

  ```json
  {
    "event_type": "deposit.received",
    "event_object": {
      "source_amount": "10000.00",
      "source_currency": "NGN",
      "destination_amount": "6.046600",
      "destination_currency": "USDC",
      "metadata": { "auto_conversion": "true", "quote_id": "qt_…", "rate": "0.000605" },
      "wallet": { "id": "wal_…", "currency": "USDC", "label": "Treasury USDC" },
      "customer": { "id": "cust_…", "type": "business", "name": "Acme Ltd" },
      "credential": { "id": "wcr_…", "currency": "NGN", "details": { "accountNumber": "…" } }
    }
  }
  ```

  The `wallet`, `customer` and `credential` blocks are new, and appear **only** on cross-currency
  deposit events. They tell you which credential was funded — including the payer-facing bank
  details of the virtual account — without a second API call. Same-currency deposit payloads are
  unchanged.

  Two things to know before you switch on handling:

  - `destination_amount` is the **quoted** amount, not the settled one. Reconcile against
    `actual_destination_amount` on `conversion.success`.
  - A cross-currency deposit **never reaches a terminal** `deposit.*` state. It stays at
    `received`, and the `conversion.*` topics carry the outcome. Do not wait for
    `deposit.confirmed` on this path.

  A deposit that breaches your customer's rolling money-in limit now emits `deposit.held` with
  `metadata.hold_reason` of `tier_limit` and a null `destination_amount` — no rate is quoted while
  it is under review. On release you receive `deposit.received` with the amount quoted at the
  then-current rate.

  ### Fixed: credential `currency` on a cross-currency credential

  `GET /v1/wallets/{id}/credentials` and `GET /v1/wallets/{id}/credentials/{credentialId}`
  reported the **wallet's** currency for every credential. On a cross-currency credential that was
  wrong — an NGN credential on a USDC wallet returned `"currency": "USDC"` alongside its NGN bank
  details. Both endpoints now return the credential's own currency. The nested `wallet.currency`
  still reports the wallet's, so you can read both. Same-currency credentials are unaffected,
  since the two values are identical there.

  <Warning>
    If you read `currency` on a cross-currency credential and relied on it meaning "the wallet's
    currency", update that code — it now means "the currency this credential accepts".
  </Warning>
</Update>

<Update label="2026-09-21" tags={["New feature"]}>
  ## Forwarding wallets send every deposit straight to your own address

  `POST /v1/wallets` now accepts a `type`. The default, `custodial`, is every wallet you have
  today: it holds a balance you can convert, offramp and transfer out of. Nothing about your
  existing wallets changes, and omitting `type` keeps current behaviour.

  The new `forwarding` type holds no balance. You give it an address you control at creation, and
  every inflow is delivered there:

  ```json
  POST /v1/wallets
  {
    "currency": "USDC",
    "type": "forwarding",
    "destination_address": "0x1111111111111111111111111111111111111111",
    "destination_chain": "base"
  }
  ```

  `destination_address` and `destination_chain` are required for `forwarding` and rejected on a
  `custodial` wallet. `destination_chain` is one of `solana`, `base` or `ethereum` and must be
  supported by the wallet's currency; the address is validated against the chain you pick. A
  forwarding wallet must use a crypto currency.

  **Both fields are permanent.** They cannot be changed after creation — create a new wallet to
  change where funds land. Please double-check the address: every future deposit to that wallet
  goes to it, and we cannot recover funds sent to an address you do not control.

  Two consequences worth knowing before you integrate:

  - A forwarding wallet's `available_balance`, `pending_balance` and `total_balance` always read
    zero, because it never holds funds.
  - It cannot be the **source** of a transfer, offramp or conversion — those are rejected with
    [`400 invalid_input`](/errors#invalid_input). It is a destination only.

  All four wallet endpoints now return `type`, `destination_address` and `destination_chain`. On a
  custodial wallet the two destination fields are `null`.
</Update>

<Update label="2026-09-18" tags={["New feature"]}>
  ## Transactions now return the amount the recipient received

  `GET /v1/transactions` and `GET /v1/transactions/{transactionId}` now return `received_amount`:
  the amount that actually reached the recipient, formatted to the destination currency's
  precision.

  This is not the same figure as `destination_amount`. `destination_amount` is the amount you were
  quoted, and on corridors that surface a transaction fee separately it is the amount *before* that
  fee — an off-ramp quoted at 110 ZAR credits the beneficiary's bank 100. If you have been showing
  `destination_amount` as the payout figure, or subtracting fee line items from it yourself,
  `received_amount` is the number you want. It is the same field, with the same meaning, already
  returned on quote, onramp, offramp, and conversion responses.

  `received_amount` is `null` until a transaction reaches a terminal success, and stays `null` on
  transactions that never succeeded, on transfers, and on deposits. **Treat `null` as "not known
  yet", never as zero** — for an in-flight transaction, `destination_amount` remains your best
  estimate of what will be delivered. Once set, the figure is retained: a payout that is later
  returned keeps the amount it originally delivered. Existing transactions were backfilled, so
  transactions that settled before this change return the field too.

  The field also appears on each entry in `related_transactions`. `destination_amount` is unchanged
  and still returned, so nothing you already key off has moved.

  <Note>
    `deposit.*` webhook payloads now include `received_amount` as well. Deposits have no payout leg,
    so it is always `null` there.
  </Note>
</Update>

<Update label="2026-09-18" tags={["New feature"]}>
  ## Wallets now tell you which customer owns them

  Wallet responses now include a `customer` object alongside the existing `customer_id`, so you no
  longer need a second call to `/v1/customers` just to show an owner's name. It is returned by
  `POST /v1/wallets`, `GET /v1/wallets`, `GET /v1/wallets/{walletId}` and
  `PATCH /v1/wallets/{walletId}`.

  The object carries `id`, `type`, `name`, `email`, `tier`, `created_at` and `updated_at` — the same
  shape already nested on transfer, onramp, offramp, and conversion responses. For KYC status,
  metadata, or the customer profile, keep using `GET /v1/customers/{customerId}`.

  `customer_id` is unchanged and still present, so nothing you already key off has moved. Wallets
  owned by your integration's default customer return a populated `customer` too — those are the
  wallets you could not previously resolve a name for, since the default customer is not listed by
  `GET /v1/customers`.
</Update>

<Update label="2026-08-25" tags={["New feature"]}>
  ## Quote by the amount the recipient receives with `target_amount`

  Onramp, offramp, and conversion quotes now accept `target_amount` as an alternative to
  `source_amount`. `target_amount` is the amount the recipient receives — the crypto credited to
  the wallet on an onramp, the fiat paid to the beneficiary on an offramp, or the amount credited
  to the destination wallet on a conversion.

  Provide **exactly one** of `source_amount` or `target_amount`. Sending both, or neither, is
  rejected with [`400 invalid_input`](/errors#invalid_input) before anything is created. When you
  send `target_amount`, we compute the required `source_amount` and return it on the quote, so the
  response always carries both `source_amount` and `destination_amount`.

  The USDC 2-decimal-place settlement limit applies only when you supply `source_amount`. A
  `target_amount`-driven quote is not constrained this way — we derive a settleable source amount
  for you.

  ## Quotes now return their fee breakdown

  Quote responses now include an optional `fee` object with a per-component `line_items` breakdown
  (`name`, `amount`, `currency`), matching the `fee` already returned on transactions. Each
  component keeps its own currency — for example a gateway fee in the source currency and a network
  fee in the destination currency. The `fee` field is omitted when a quote carries no fee
  components.
</Update>

<Update label="2026-07-27" tags={["Behavior change"]}>
  ## USDC amounts are limited to 2 decimal places

  USDC settles in whole US cents, so USDC amounts now accept at most **2 significant decimal
  places**. Trailing zeros are fine — `2.980000` is accepted, `2.980228` is rejected with
  [`400 invalid_amount_precision`](/errors#invalid_amount_precision).

  This applies to `source_amount` on offramp quotes and conversion quotes, and to `amount` on
  transfers. The request is rejected before anything is created, so a rejected call leaves no
  quote and moves no funds.

  If you derive amounts from a balance or a destination-anchored price, round to 2 decimal places
  before sending them.

  ## Offramps fail immediately when funding fails

  An offramp whose funding transfer cannot be completed now fails as soon as that becomes known,
  with the new failure reason [`funding_failed`](/api-reference/failure-reasons). Previously the
  offramp stayed in `processing` until its provider quote expired, roughly an hour later.

  No funds move in this case. Branch on `failure_reason` to retry with a new quote — and expect
  the `offramp.failed` webhook sooner than before.
</Update>
