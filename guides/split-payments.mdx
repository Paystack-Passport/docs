---
title: "Split payments"
description: "Share what an onramp or conversion delivers between several of your wallets, for example a marketplace paying its sellers."
---

A **split** shares the amount an onramp or a conversion delivers between several wallets. The
typical case is a marketplace: a buyer pays once, each seller's wallet gets its share, and the
marketplace's own wallet keeps the rest.

You attach a split to a **quote**. When the payment settles, every recipient is credited in the
same step, and you can follow each share in the transactions API and through webhooks.

## How a split works

- The quote's **destination wallet** receives the payment, as it does without a split. It keeps
  the **remainder**: whatever the shares don't assign.
- Each **share** names a recipient wallet and either a `percentage` of the delivered amount or a
  flat `amount`.
- All wallets (recipients and destination) must be **custodial** wallets of your integration that
  hold the quote's destination currency. The destination wallet can't also be a recipient.

| Type | Each share is | Needs | If the payment falls short |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `percentage` | A percentage of the amount actually delivered. Percentages add up to 100 or less. | Nothing extra. | Every share shrinks in proportion. |
| `flat` | A fixed amount. | `target_amount` on the quote; the shares must not exceed it. | If the delivered amount can't cover the shares, nothing is split (see below). |

### Provider fees: `fee_bearer`

A conversion into a fiat currency can arrive with a provider fee already taken off. Shares are
always worked out on the **gross** amount, before the fee, so "60%" or "ZAR 1,000" means the same
with or without a fee. `fee_bearer` decides who gives up the fee:

| `fee_bearer` | Who absorbs the fee |
| --- | --- |
| `merchant` (default) | The destination wallet's remainder. Recipients get their full share. |
| `all_proportional` | Every share, the remainder included, in proportion to its size. |

Each wallet is credited its share **net** of its part of the fee. Crypto payments carry no fee, so
`fee_bearer` changes nothing there. If the shares can't be covered once the fee is taken off, the
split is skipped with `insufficient_delivered_amount`.

## Create a saved split, or send one inline

Save a split you reuse with [`POST /v1/splits`](/api-reference/splits/create-a-split):

```json
POST /v1/splits
{
  "name": "Order 1042 sellers",
  "type": "percentage",
  "fee_bearer": "merchant",
  "shares": [
    { "wallet_id": "wal_SELLER_A", "percentage": "60.00" },
    { "wallet_id": "wal_SELLER_B", "percentage": "25.00" }
  ]
}
```

Then pass its `id` as `split_code` when you create the quote. For a one-off, send the same
object inline as `split` instead. Send one or the other, not both.

```json
POST /v1/onramps/quotes
{
  "wallet_id": "wal_MARKETPLACE",
  "source_currency": "NGN",
  "source_network": "bank",
  "source_amount": "160000",
  "split_code": "spl_01HXYZ…"
}
```

`fee_bearer` is optional and defaults to `merchant`. You can change it later with
`PATCH /v1/splits/{split_id}`.

The quote keeps a copy of the split, `fee_bearer` included. Editing or deactivating a saved split
later never changes a quote that already uses it.

<Note>
  Splits are available on onramps, on conversions whose destination is a crypto currency, and on
  conversions into a fiat currency enabled for splits (otherwise the quote returns
  `invalid_input`). Offramps and transfers can't be split.
</Note>

## Follow the split

Onramps and conversions carry a `split` block (`null` when the quote had no split):

| `status` | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `in_progress` | The split hasn't been applied yet, or isn't decided for every recipient. The payment that applies it can arrive after `onramp.success` or `conversion.success`. |
| `completed` | Every recipient has an outcome. A held share counts as decided: check each leg. |
| `skipped` | The split couldn't apply (`reason`: `insufficient_delivered_amount` or `shares_round_to_zero`). The whole amount stayed on the destination wallet. |

The block also carries the split's `fee_bearer`. `legs` lists every wallet credited, the
destination's remainder included (`is_remainder: true`), each with its `amount` (what the wallet
received, net of its part of any provider fee), its `fee` (that part; zero when there was no fee)
and its `status`: `processing`, `success`, `held` or `failed`.

<Note>
  If an onramp or conversion ends without a payment the split could apply to (for example it
  failed or was returned), its split stays `in_progress` and no `split.completed` event follows.
</Note>

### Held shares

Each recipient's share counts toward that recipient's own limits, just like a deposit. A share
can be `held`, with a `hold_reason`:

- `tier_limit`: the recipient's customer is over their limit. The share is released
  automatically when their limit allows it, for example after they're verified at a higher tier.
- `payin_frozen` or `payin_disabled`: the destination wallet's pay-in is blocked.

The other shares settle normally.

### Webhooks

Subscribe your endpoint to the `split` category:

- `split.completed` fires once per payment, when its split is `completed` or `skipped`.
- `split.leg_released` fires when a held share is released.

See [Webhook events → Splits](/webhooks/events#splits).

### Transactions

The payment's own `deposit` transaction still shows the full amount credited to the destination
wallet, and a provider fee still has its own `fee` transaction. Each recipient's share is a child
transaction of type `split`, from the destination wallet to the recipient, for the amount the
recipient received; when the recipient bore part of a fee, its `metadata.fee_amount` holds that
part. List them with
`GET /v1/transactions?type=split&include_related=true`, optionally with the recipient's
`wallet_id`.

## Worked example

A buyer pays for an order worth 100 USDC on your marketplace. Seller A gets 60%, seller B 25%,
and your marketplace wallet keeps the rest.

| Wallet | Share | Credited |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seller A | 60% | 60 USDC |
| Seller B | 25% | 25 USDC |
| Marketplace (destination) | remainder | 15 USDC |

When the payment settles you receive `onramp.success`, then `split.completed` with all three legs
`success`. If seller B is over their limit, their leg is `held` (`tier_limit`) and the other two
are `success`; `split.leg_released` follows when B's share is released.

### With a provider fee

A USDC → ZAR conversion settles ZAR 6,000.00 with a ZAR 100.00 provider fee. Seller A gets 60%.

| Wallet | Gross share | `merchant`: fee / credited | `all_proportional`: fee / credited |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seller A | 3,600.00 | 0.00 / 3,600.00 | 60.00 / 3,540.00 |
| Marketplace (destination) | 2,400.00 | 100.00 / 2,300.00 | 40.00 / 2,360.00 |

Either way the wallets receive ZAR 5,900.00 in total: the amount that arrived after the fee.

## Limits

- Wallets must be custodial, belong to your integration and hold the destination currency.
- 1 to 20 shares per split.
- Conversions can be split into a crypto destination, or into a fiat currency enabled for splits.
